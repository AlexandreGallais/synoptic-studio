# Interaction

How the user acts on shapes in the editors: tools, selection, node editing, snapping, alignment, tree and history.

## 1. Tools

| Tool | French | Role | Functional reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selector | Sélecteur | select, move, resize objects | Inkscape, Figma |
| Node tool | Édition de nœuds | select and modify the vertices of a shape | Inkscape (nodes), Figma (vector edit) |
| Rectangle | Rectangle | create a rectangle | — |
| Polygon | Polygone régulier | create a polygon with n corners | Inkscape Star/Polygon |
| Text | Texte | create a text | — |
| Port | Port | place a connection point (Symbol Editor) | — |

No pen tool and no freehand drawing (see `shapes.md` §3).

**Keys editable** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22): every shortcut and modifier key (Shift, Ctrl, Delete, the arrows…) can be changed by the user, simply, in a standard place for settings; the defaults follow Figma's. For E20.

**The default keys** (SP-027, 2026-10-22; Figma's where its pages were read, the agent's choice where they were not, for the review's summary (AUD-039, F37 having no validation of its own); each editor's keys and the reasons in [note 0028](../research/notes/0028-f37-select-shapes.md)). Ctrl stands for Cmd on macOS; a key pressed while a field of the panel has the focus edits the field, never the drawing, a slider of the color picker counting as a field (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, AUD-041); but for undo and redo: a field with nothing typed since its value was applied leaves Ctrl + Z, Ctrl + Shift + Z and Ctrl + Y to the drawing, a field being typed in keeps them (SP-029's answer 9, the choice left to the agent by the Product Owner, 2026-10-23; §7, Undo and redo in the playground).

**Adding a shape** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22, F35), as Figma does:

- a tool chosen, a click on the drawing adds a shape 100 × 100 centred on the point clicked, on integers; a text fits its content instead, a new text empty and centred by its frame as it is typed (below, US-075);
- **on integers** (agent's choice, US-063): the top-left of the shape's box, before it turns, is the point clicked less half the box's size, each coordinate rounded to the nearest integer, a tie rounded up (toward +∞), as CSS rounds (`nearestInteger`); a point clicked between two integers once the view's scale is applied, at (250.5, 140.4), places a shape 100 × 100 at (201, 90); a new text's frame, 1 × 38.71875 clicked at (250, 140), sits at (250, 121) (`centredPosition`);
- **a text without metrics** (agent's choice, US-063, written at AUD-037): a text placed while its font has no metrics, no font being available yet (US-056), is not measured, its block taken as 0 × 0: the top-left of its box is the point clicked, rounded as above, (250.5, 140.6) giving (251, 141), since a size it does not have cannot be centred;
- **a kept text without metrics** (agent's choice, AUD-037): a text kept in the drawing whose font's metrics are no longer read, a family read ahead whose files could not be read again leaving the session (US-061) while Inter is not drawn (US-056), is drawn and exported with the metrics it was last drawn with, so that the shapes already there do not change;
- a press, a drag and a release draw the shape's box instead, the shape following the pointer until the release; a text dragged sits in a box of that size; with Shift held the box stays square, its side the larger of the two distances dragged, so that a round shape is a circle (Product Owner, 2026-10-22; Shift as Figma does, Inkscape uses Ctrl);
- **the box dragged** (agent's choice, US-064, `draggedBox`): the press and the pointer each rounded to the nearest integer, a tie rounded up, as a click is (`nearestInteger`); the box runs from the smaller to the larger of each coordinate, in any direction: (10, 10) to (130, 90) and (130, 90) to (10, 10) both give 120 × 80 at (10, 10), and (10.4, 9.6) to (130.5, 90.49) gives 121 × 80 at (10, 10); square, the side is laid from the press toward the pointer, down or right when the pointer is level with the press: (130, 90) to (10, 10) gives 120 × 120 at (10, −30); Shift pressed or released during the drag changes the shape at once, and a text never stays square; the new shape takes its kind's settings, straight, as a click's does, and the selector is armed again at the release;
- **a release within 3 pixels of the press** is a click (SP-030's answer 8, 2026-10-23, superseding the release on the press's integer point of US-063): while the pointer stays within 3 screen pixels of the press, at any zoom, the pixels divided by the view's scale (`isDragged`), the release places the shape centred on the point pressed; once the pointer goes further, the press is a drag until the release, its shape drawn, placed as a click if the pointer comes back to the press's integer point (agent's choice, US-064). At 10 %, a tremble of 1 pixel, 10 units, still places 100 × 100 centred on the press; at 800 %, a drag of 7 pixels across from (10, 10) draws a rectangle 1 × 0 at (10, 10), one of 3 pixels places 100 × 100 at (−40, −40);
- **a width or a height of 0** (agent's choice, US-064): a drag straight across or straight down adds the shape as drawn, its width or its height 0, as the model accepts (Q15): (10, 10) to (10.3, 90) adds a shape 0 × 80 at (10, 10), which draws nothing visible until a side is typed; Shift makes it a square of the distance dragged; a text gets a box of that size;
- the new shape is drawn above the others, the drawing an ordered list of shapes, the last drawn on top, flat until E13; above all of them, the shapes above a shape picked from the middle of the drawing included (§2, A click with the Selector).
- **the toolbar** (agent's choice, US-063, as Figma does): a tool's button arms the tool and leaves the shape the panel edits as it is; a click on the drawing then places the tool's shape, the panel edits it, and the **selector** is armed again; with the selector armed, a click on the drawing places nothing, it picks the shape under it (§2, A click with the Selector);
- a shape refused, a value out of its range, is not kept when another is placed: only the shapes drawn are (agent's choice, US-063);
- in the playground, Reset and each step of the guided test start the drawing again as the page opens: the shapes placed and the settings each kind kept are forgotten, the Text tool's fields written back to the page's, so that the next text is Inter 32 Regular (agent's choice, US-063; the text's fields since AUD-037).
- **For later**: duplicating a shape, the offset of its first move remembered for the next copies, as Figma does, so that copies never pile up (Product Owner, 2026-10-22; not in E02).

**What a new shape starts with** (agent's choice, SP-026, 2026-10-22, the choice left to research by the Product Owner; to confirm at VAL-036; sources and reasons in [note 0027](../research/notes/0027-f35-what-a-new-shape-starts-with.md)):

- its box and place come from the click or the drag, as above, and it starts straight, its angle 0;
- every other setting is the one of the **last shape of its kind** the panel showed, so that a triangle placed again is the same triangle, as Inkscape's shape tools do (`REF-INKSCAPE-TUTORIAL-SHAPES`): a rectangle its corner radii, a polygon its corners, radii, stretched option and anchor, a text every option but its content and box — font, size, weight, color, spacing, decorations, case, line height, trim, background, ellipsis —, as Figma and Inkscape give a new text the last style used (`REF-INKSCAPE-1-2-TOOL-STYLE`, `REF-FIGMA-FORUM-TEXT-DEFAULT`);
- one memory per kind: a rectangle's values never reach a polygon, nor the other way round; before any shape of a kind, the values the page opens with (a radius of 10, 6 corners, the anchor at the center, Inter 32 Regular);
- a shape's settings are kept when the panel leaves it, for a new shape or because it became a free shape (US-063): a polygon set to 3 corners, then converted, gives the next polygon its 3 corners;
- a new text has no content of its own: the designer clicks, then types, its Content field taking the focus (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, superseding SP-026's `Text`, US-075), as Figma and Inkscape start an empty text typed on the drawing;
- **a new text being typed** (agent's choices, US-075, for the review's summary, F40 having no validation of its own): from its placing until its Content field is left with a content typed, or the panel leaves it; while its content is empty it shows a frame 1 wide and one line tall, its line height (`textLineHeight`), dashed as a text's frame, where its first character will go (`readShownTextBlock`); a click centres that frame on the point on integers, so that its first line is centred on the point and the text grows to the right as it is typed: Inter Regular 32 clicked at (250, 140), its line (1984 + 494) × 32 / 2048 = 38.71875 tall, has its top-left at (250 − 0.5, 140 − 19.359375) = (249.5, 120.640625), on (250, 121); its block stays empty, so that it draws and exports nothing; a text dragged shows its box; only a new text shows that frame, an empty text otherwise none (`shapes.md` §3, Text, Empty text);
- **a new text left empty** (agent's choice, US-075): its Content field left empty, it stays being typed, so that its size or its font may be set before its content; the panel leaving it still empty — a click on the drawing, another shape placed, Escape, Ctrl + A, an undo — removes it, as a refused shape is not kept, the drawing back as it was, so that no step is taken (§7);
- example: a polygon set to 3 corners, a radius of 4 and its anchor at the bottom center, then a rectangle set to a radius of 0; the polygon tool clicked at (250, 140) adds a triangle of radius 4 anchored at the bottom center, its box 100 × 100 at (200, 90), straight; the rectangle tool clicked next adds a rectangle of radius 0;
- why not Figma's fixed defaults (a rectangle 100 × 100, a polygon a triangle, `REF-FIGMA-SHAPE-TOOLS`): Figma gets alike shapes by duplicating, components and styles, none of them in E02; the panel already shows the values, so what is shown is what is placed. A default set by a command (Sketch, Affinity Designer) and a reset to the page's values (Inkscape's button) are left out.

## 2. Selector

- Click: select an object; Shift + click: add / remove; Ctrl + click (Cmd on macOS) is kept for picking a shape inside a group when E13 brings groups, as Figma (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, SP-027, superseding for the drawing the rule of 2026-10-11, which the list of points keeps). No range on the drawing.
- **The selection rectangle** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22, SP-027): it takes the shapes whose painted area it touches, as a click picks; with Alt held, only those it encloses. Each shape selected shows its band, and a dashed box surrounds the whole selection. A shape whose points lie on one line though its box has an area paints nothing and a click never picks it, but a rectangle touches it by its line and encloses it by its box, so that it can still be selected and removed (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, AUD-039).
  - **How** (EN-144, `shapesTouchedByBox`, `shapesEnclosedByBox`, `DERIV-box-overlap`): on each shape's contour as drawn, as under the pointer, the rectangle touches a shape when a piece of its contour has a point in the rectangle, or, none having one, the rectangle's top-left corner is in its painted area, the rectangle then lying inside the shape; it encloses a shape when the straight box of its contour, its arcs and cubic sides taken by their extremes, lies within the rectangle. A from (0, 0) to (100, 100), B from (150, 0) to (250, 100) and the circle C in the box from (300, 0) to (400, 100): the rectangle from (−10, −10) to (200, 110) touches A and B and encloses A alone; the one from (390, 90) to (420, 120) touches none, its nearest point to C's center (350, 50) lying 56.6 away, beyond the radius 50. A text is taken by its frame, as under the pointer.
  - **Its outline counts** (agent's choice, EN-144): the rectangle's sides and the shapes' contours are included, so that a rectangle meeting a shape only along a side, or a corner, touches it, and one drawn exactly along a shape's sides encloses it, the contour's box compared within `EPSILON` (ADR-0003), and the rectangle widened by `EPSILON` on every side for touching (`widenedBox`, `DERIV-box-overlap` step 10, AUD-039), so that a side the contour computes with a rounding error is touched as it is enclosed: the Polygon tool's square, 4 corners of radius 10, 100 × 100 at (0, 0), its left side at x ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁵, is touched by the rectangle from (−10, 40) to (0, 60). A is touched by the rectangle from (100, 50) to (200, 60) and enclosed by the one from (0, 0) to (100, 100). The other way was to leave out the outline, a shape then needing a rectangle one unit larger.
  - **A shape without area** (agent's choice, EN-144): a shape or a text whose box has a width or a height of 0 is touched when the rectangle meets the outline its corners join, with no tolerance, the rectangle having an area of its own, and enclosed when its corners are: a shape 0 × 80 at (10, 10) is touched by the rectangle from (5, 40) to (15, 50), not by the one from (12, 0) to (20, 100), 2 away from its line though a click there picks it. The other way was the 4 screen pixels of a click, which would take a line the rectangle does not reach.
  - **Its drag** (agent's choice, US-069): with the Selector armed and Shape mode, a press of the main button on an empty place of the drawing, no shape under it as a click reaches one (Under the pointer), starts the rectangle; the point pressed and the pointer are each rounded to the nearest integer, as a tool's drag rounds them (§1, `draggedBox`), the rectangle running from the smaller to the larger of each coordinate, in any direction; it shows once the pointer has gone further than 3 screen pixels from the press (SP-030's answer 8) and goes at the release, when the shapes it touches are selected, or with Alt held at the release those it encloses (Alt read at the release, not at the press, agent's choice, US-069), one alone loaded into the panel as a click loads it; with Shift held at the release, they are added to the shapes selected, none taken out, so that a rectangle taking none keeps the selection, as Shift + click on the empty drawing does (F37's criterion 3; none taken out, agent's choice, `shapeSelectionAfterBox`, as Shift + click never takes out a shape it does not hit). A, B and the circle C as above, C selected: the rectangle pressed at (−10, −10) and released at (200, 110), or the other way, selects A and B; with Alt, A alone; from (390, 90) to (420, 120), none; from (110, 10) to (140, 90), between A and B, none, or with Shift the selection kept; A selected, the one from (140, −10) to (260, 110) with Shift selects A and B, without Shift B alone. A drag the browser cancels selects nothing. The other ways were the press's exact point, a fraction, which a tool's drag does not keep, and, with Shift, taking out the shapes taken that were selected, as Shift + click takes out the one it hits.
  - **A drag is never a click** (agent's choice, US-069): a press with the Selector whose pointer has gone further than 3 screen pixels from it, at any zoom (SP-030's answer 8, 2026-10-23, superseding its integer point), is a drag until the release, even released back on it, and the click the release brings picks nothing; so a press on a shape draws no rectangle but moves the shapes selected (How the shapes selected move, US-072): pressed on A at (50, 50) and released over B at (200, 50), A is selected alone and moved there, the click picking nothing; released at (52, 51) at 100 %, √5 = 2.24 pixels from the press, it is a click and selects A. In Points mode, which edits one shape, a drag draws no rectangle either and selects nothing. The other way was the click at the release, which picked the shape under it wherever the press was.
  - **Shown** (agent's choice, US-069): in the layer above the drawing, never in the export, a light tint of the outlines' color over its area, its outline a band 1 screen pixel wide centred on its sides, in the outlines' color, a fill with a hole, never a native stroke, divided by the view's scale; a rectangle no wider or no higher than the band is the band alone. The rectangle from (−10, −10) to (200, 110) at scale 1 is outlined from (−10.5, −10.5) to (200.5, 110.5) outside and from (−9.5, −9.5) to (199.5, 109.5) inside. The shape under the pointer is not outlined meanwhile, the rectangle telling what is taken. The other ways were the dashed line of the box around several shapes, which the rectangle would be taken for, and an outline without a tint.
- **The panel for several shapes** (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, SP-027, F38): Mixed where the values differ; a value with a sign is added to each shape's own (`+50` in W: 100 gives 150, 50 gives 100), one without sets each to it (460 makes each 460 wide); a text fitting its content keeps its size.
  - **Each shape's values** (US-070, `shapePanelValues`, `textPanelValues`): X and Y are the top-left of its straight box, the smallest upright box around its contour as drawn, as for one shape (ADR-0035, `shapeStraightBox`), W and H its own width and height; a text is taken by its frame, its block or its box when it sits in one, as under the pointer: X and Y those of its straight box (`textStraightBox`), W and H its frame's (`textFrame`). A sharp, 100 × 50 at (10, 20), turned by 30° about its middle (60, 45), has X 4.19873, Y −1.65064, W 100, H 50, its half sizes 50 cos 30° + 25 sin 30° = 55.80127 across and 50 sin 30° + 25 cos 30° = 46.65064 down; a text whose block is 80 × 30 at (300, 20), turned by 90°, has X 325, Y −5, W 80, H 30.
  - **Common or Mixed** (US-070, `commonPanelValues`): a value is common when every shape's lies within `EPSILON` of the first shape's (ADR-0003, agent's choice), the first one then shown as one shape's is, with the SVG's decimals; otherwise the field is empty with the placeholder Mixed, as the radius of vertices of different radii (`shapes.md` §2). A at (10, 20), 100 × 50, and B at (200, 20), 40 × 40, show X Mixed, Y 20, W Mixed, H Mixed. The other way was comparing the values as shown, to 5 decimals, which the model, below the SVG's writing, does not hold.
  - **A value typed** (US-071, `parsePanelValue`, `panelValueAfterEdit`): an integer, white space around it ignored, as a typed coordinate is (ADR-0003, ADR-0034); with a sign, `+50` or `-20`, it is added to each shape's own value in that field, without one each shape is set to it, as Figma (`REF-FIGMA-ADJUST`). A and B as above: `+50` in W makes A 150 wide and B 90, `-20` in X moves A to x −10 and B to x 180; 460 in W makes both 460 wide, 50 in X puts both at x 50. A fraction, an empty field, any other text, or an integer past the safe ones (2⁵³ − 1) is refused. A negative value is relative, so that a negative x is reached from the shapes' own: −20 cannot set them all at x −20, a consequence of the sign's rule.
  - **A value refused changes none** (agent's choice, US-071): a width or height that would fall below 0 for one shape refuses the value for all of them, so that a value goes to every shape selected or to none: `-50` in W leaves A at 100 and B at 40, B's 40 − 50 being below 0. A width or height above 100 000 is capped at 100 000, as one shape's field caps it (Q20): 200 000 typed in W makes each 100 000 wide. The other ways were changing the shapes that can take the value, and refusing a size above 100 000.
  - **Each shape set** (US-071, `shapeAfterPanelEdit`): X or Y moves the shape so that its straight box starts at its new value, its position, the top-left of its box before it turns, going by the difference, as one shape's X and Y (ADR-0035): A turned by 30°, its straight box at x 4.19873, goes to x 55.80127 for 50 typed in X. W or H sets its own width or height, a turned shape keeping its turned top-left corner, as one shape's (US-019, `positionAfterResize`): A turned by 30°, its turned top-left corner at (29.19873, −1.65064), made 150 wide by `+50`, has its position at (6.65064, 32.5), its middle at (81.65064, 57.5). Its corners, radii and other settings are kept, the settings of each kind (§1, What a new shape starts with) unchanged. A shape 0 × 0, whose contour has no piece, is taken by the point its corners lie on: 0 × 0 at (10, 20) shows X 10, Y 20, and 50 in X puts it at x 50.
  - **A text** (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, SP-027; how, agent's choice, US-071, `textAfterPanelEdit`): X or Y moves it by its straight box, as a shape; W or H sets the width or height of its box when it sits in one, its turned top-left corner kept (ADR-0057), and leaves a text fitting its content as it is, which never refuses the value, having no width to set: `-100` in W leaves such a text as it is while making B, 40 wide, refuse it. A text never measured, left out of the values, is left as it is. A text turned by 23° in a box 100 × 20 at (47, 61), the box made 120 wide, has its position at (46.20505, 64.90731), ADR-0057's example.
  - **Shown** (agent's choice, US-070): four fields of their own, X, Y, Width and Height, under the panel's title, the shape's other fields staying hidden, so that the fields of the shape the panel edited, which a shape placed next takes its settings from (§1, What a new shape starts with), are never written for several; hidden with one shape selected, or none. A value typed in one is applied on Enter or when the field is left, as one shape's fields (§3, Moving a vertex), never while it is typed, the fields then showing the shapes' values again, so that a value refused is put back (US-071); they are text fields, which keep the `+` of a relative value that a number field drops. A text never measured, which is not drawn, is left out of the values, as out of the dashed box; with no shape left to speak for, each field is empty, without a placeholder. The other ways were the shape's own fields, kept shown, and fields shown disabled.
- **Two layers** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22, F35): the drawing's SVG holds only the shapes and receives the clicks; a transparent SVG above it, which the pointer passes through, draws the outline of the shape under the pointer, the selection and every other help, so that the drawing is never polluted by them.
- **Under the pointer** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22, F36): the top shape whose painted area holds the point, only what is visible, so that shapes are picked precisely: a click in the cut corner of a rounded rectangle, or inside a circle's box but outside the circle, picks nothing; computed in the model (ADR-0005, EN-143).
  - **How** (EN-143, `shapeAtPoint`, `DERIV-painted-area`): on each shape's contour as drawn, turned, its corners rounded and its sides curved, the point is in a shape when its contour winds around it, by the nonzero rule the SVG fills it with (`REF-SVG2-FILL-RULE`); the last shape drawn that holds it is the one under the pointer, none when no shape holds it. A, a box from (0, 0) to (100, 100), then B, from (50, 50) to (150, 150): (75, 75) gives B, (25, 25) A, (200, 200) none; a rectangle 100 × 100 of radius 20 holds (8, 8) but not (3, 3).
  - **A text** (agent's choice, EN-143): it is under the pointer anywhere in its frame — its block, or its box when it sits in one — turned with it, as its straight box is taken (`textStraightBox`), not only on its letters: a text is picked between its words and its lines, and in the room its box leaves. The other ways were the letters' outlines, which the model does not hold, and the lines' backgrounds.
  - **A shape without area** (agent's choice, EN-143): a shape or a text whose box has a width or a height of 0 paints nothing (§1, a width or a height of 0); it is under the pointer when the pointer is within **4 screen pixels** of the outline its corners join, those pixels divided by the view's scale, so that the reach does not change with the zoom, as snapping's tolerance (§4): at scale 1, a shape 0 × 80 at (10, 10) is under (12, 50), 2 away, not under (15, 50), 5 away. The other ways were never to pick it, leaving it to a list of the shapes, or to take its box widened to a side of 1. A shape whose box has an area but whose points lie on one line (§3, Degenerate results) paints nothing and is not picked.
  - **The outline** (agent's choice, US-065): with the Selector armed, the shape under the pointer is outlined in the layer above the drawing by a band **2 screen pixels** wide centred on its contour as drawn — a text's frame, the outline a flat shape's corners join —, the stroke SVG 2 draws with round joins (`REF-SVG2-PAINT`) but filled, never a native stroke, a curve followed by chords at most 2 screen pixels long (`contourBand`); both lengths divided by the view's scale. None while a tool is armed, a click then placing a shape rather than picking one, nor once the pointer leaves the drawing; drawn again with the drawing, so that it follows a shape changed under a pointer standing still; never in the export. The other ways were the shape's box, which outlines more than is picked, and a tint over its area, which changes its color.
- **A click with the Selector** (Product Owner, 2026-10-22, F36): it selects the shape under the pointer alone, which keeps its place in the drawing order, and the panel shows and edits its values; a click on the empty drawing selects none.
  - **Its place kept** (US-067, `splitDrawing`): the drawing is split around the shape the panel edits, the shapes drawn before it below, those drawn after it above; a shape placed next goes on top of all. A, B and C drawn in that order, C edited: a click on A edits A, still drawn under B and C, and a rectangle placed next is drawn above C.
  - **Which click picks** (agent's choice, US-067): a click picks only when its press was made with the Selector armed, so that the click ending a drag with a tool, or placing a shape, after which the Selector is armed again, never picks a shape too; a drag from (200, 150) to (100, 60) released over another shape edits the shape drawn. Nor does the click ending a drag with the Selector, whose pointer went further than 3 pixels from the press (US-069, A drag is never a click). A click on the shape selected loads it again, no point selected. The shape the panel leaves keeps its settings as the last of its kind, unless it was refused (§1, What a new shape starts with).
  - **The selection shown** (agent's choice, US-067): the shape the panel edits is outlined in the layer above the drawing by the hover's band, 2 screen pixels wide about its contour as drawn (`contourBand`), in the same color, whichever tool is armed, as Figma keeps a selection shown while a tool is chosen; none while it is refused or none is selected; never in the export. The other ways were its box with handles, which come with moving and resizing, and a band of another color.
  - **None selected** (agent's choice, US-067): every shape stays drawn, in the order drawn; the panel hides its fields, its title says no shape is selected and its status how to select one or add one; a tool then places a shape as usual, on top of all.
- **Several shapes selected** (Product Owner, 2026-10-23, SP-027, F37): Shift + click adds the shape under the pointer, or takes it out when it is selected; a plain click, or Ctrl + click, selects it alone (`shapeSelectionAfterClick`, the rule of the vertices' handles, §3, without Ctrl). A, B and C drawn in that order, A selected: Shift + click on B selects A and B; Shift + click on B again leaves A alone; Ctrl + click on C selects C alone.

## 5. Colors, align and distribute

### Colors

F16; decided at F05's refinement, round 2, 2026-10-14; the rules marked "confirmed at VAL-016" were read from that decision by the agent at SP-017, US-035 and AUD-017, 2026-10-15, confirmed at VAL-016; ADR-0056, `DERIV-color-spaces`.

- **A color**: a hexadecimal value and an opacity, an integer from 0 to 100 %, in two fields always shown beside its swatch, for a quick copy and paste, as Figma's; RGB fields and CSS color names are not offered.
- **Uses**: it serves the text's color today, its background (the agent's reading of F16's fourth criterion, confirmed at VAL-016), and its underline's and strike-through's (US-041), the shapes' fills and strokes with E04.
- **Typed or pasted** (confirmed at VAL-016):
  - with or without `#`, in upper or lower case, white space around it ignored, in 6 digits, in 3 digits each doubled (`f80` gives `#ff8800`), or in 8 or 4 digits whose last pair or digit is the alpha, which sets the opacity to the nearest percent (`#0000ffcc` gives `#0000ff` and 80 %, `#F80C` gives `#ff8800` and 80 %);
  - it is kept as `#` and six lower-case digits, the form of CSS's HTML-compatible serialization (`REF-CSS-COLOR-4`, §16.2.1);
  - the model accepts that form only, so every reader of a color (a field typed or pasted, data loaded) turns it to lower case first, and a color of F05 or F14 written in upper case is turned to lower case when read (agent, SP-017 audit, confirmed at VAL-016);
  - anything else is refused when the field is left or Enter pressed: the field shows again the color it had when it got the focus, or the last one it kept, and a line under it says why (US-035, confirmed at VAL-016);
  - while it is typed, the text is drawn with what the field holds, refused as any other field until it is a color;
  - a color copied is six digits; the opacity is copied from its own field.
- **The underline's and the strike-through's colors** (US-041, the agent's reading, confirmed at VAL-016):
  - each has the same swatch, fields and picker as the text's, with a box "follows the text's color", ticked as the page opens;
  - while ticked, its fields show the text's color and opacity and the stroke is drawn in them;
  - a hexadecimal or an opacity committed (the field left or Enter pressed) and accepted, or picked, unticks it, a value refused or being typed leaving it ticked, the stroke keeping that color when the text's changes;
  - ticked again, it follows the text's color again (`shapes.md`, Text).
- **The picker** (US-035, AUD-017, confirmed at VAL-016; integers since ADR-0058, US-040):
  - it opens on a click on the swatch and closes on another click on it, on Escape or on a click outside;
  - it holds a hue slider from 0 to 359°, holding whole degrees (`#ff8000` at 30°; a hue rounding to 360 is shown 0; Product Owner, 2026-10-15, ADR-0058), and an opacity slider from 0 to 100 %;
  - it holds a plane in HSL or in HSB, a choice between the two, HSB first (the agent's own choice, no source read, confirmed at VAL-016), the plane's three values (hue, saturation, lightness or brightness) as numbers, and the hexadecimal and opacity fields, so the hexadecimal translation is always shown;
  - in the playground the picker opens under its color's fields, which stay in sight, a click on them does not close it, and Escape gives the focus back to the swatch;
  - another model chosen reads the color's values again in it, its own hue, the same in both models, a grey keeping the hue the picker holds (`#7e7f80`, its hue typed 200 in HSB, shows HSL 210°, 1 %, 50 %, its own hue).
- **The plane** shows the colors of the hue chosen: the saturation across, from 0 at the left to 100 % at the right, the lightness or brightness up, from 0 at the bottom to 100 % at the top; a click or a drag picks both (confirmed at VAL-016, `DERIV-color-spaces`, step 10).
- **The values shown and typed** in HSL and HSB are whole numbers, never a decimal (`#ff8000` is HSL 30°, 100 %, 50 % and HSB 30°, 100 %, 100 %):
  - the hexadecimal stays the truth: a color typed or pasted in hexadecimal is kept exactly as typed and never altered afterwards, and the values keep their decimals inside, unshown;
  - the picker's inputs reach fewer colors than the hexadecimal does, which is accepted (Product Owner, 2026-10-15);
  - editing one value rebuilds the color from that typed integer and the exact values of the two others, computed from the hexadecimal, so that editing one never moves the others (Product Owner, 2026-10-15, ADR-0058, superseding ADR-0056's one decimal; US-040);
  - so `#336699` is HSB 210°, 66.66667 %, 60 %, shown 210, 67, 60; its brightness typed 61 gives `#34689c`, where the three shown integers would give `#33679c`;
  - a value typed equal to the one shown changes nothing (agent's reading, confirmed at VAL-016);
  - while the picker is open it holds the values given, and rebuilds each edit against the color its values were last read from (when it opened, a hexadecimal typed, another model chosen);
  - a value equal to the one read stands for its exact value, a value typed stays as typed, so that H 30, S 100, L 50 typed one after the other on `#1e293b` give `#ff8000` (agent's reading, ADR-0059, US-040, confirmed at VAL-016);
  - the picker's look is polished later.
- **From HSL or HSB to the hexadecimal** (US-035, confirmed at VAL-016):
  - a value typed or picked in HSL or HSB becomes the hexadecimal by rounding each of red, green and blue to the nearest integer, a tie rounded up; the hexadecimal and the opacity are what the text keeps;
  - a grey has no hue of its own: it shows 0°, while an open picker keeps the hue its slider shows, so that a grey made lighter keeps it;

## 7. Navigation and history

- Zoom and pan (F42): the view of the drawing, a scale and an origin kept outside the drawing, both SVGs given its `viewBox` (ADR-0098; §7, The view).
- Undo / redo (**Q7**, settled by the Product Owner on 2026-10-22): one step per finished action — a drag at its release, whatever its path; a value typed when Enter is pressed or the field left; a text when its editing is left; an addition, a deletion. ### The view

How the drawing is zoomed and panned (ADR-0098, accepted on 2026-10-23, SP-030's answer 1; `src/model/view/`, `DERIV-view-zoom`). The examples use a canvas of 1 200 × 800 CSS pixels:

- **The view** is a scale, CSS pixels per user unit, and an origin, the point of the drawing at the canvas's top-left; it is not in the drawing, nor in the history, nor in the export, and no shape's path or coordinate changes with it. The page opens at scale 1, origin (−40, −40), as before F42, and Reset goes back to it (SP-030's answer 6).
- **Its `viewBox`**, given to both SVGs, the drawing and the layer above it: `(o.x, o.y, W / s, H / s)`, the canvas's aspect, so one scale and nothing centred (`REF-SVG2-COORDS`, §8.2): at the opening view `-40 -40 1200 800`; at scale 2, origin (180, 130), `180 130 600 400` (`shownViewBox`).
- **The point under the pointer** is the origin plus the pointer's offset on the canvas divided by the scale, `o + p / s`, computed, never read back from the page (ADR-0005): at scale 2, origin (180, 130), the offset (440, 340) is over (400, 300); at the opening view, (40, 40) over (0, 0) (`pointUnderPointer`).
- **A zoom about a point** keeps the point under it: to the scale `s′` about the offset `p`, the origin becomes `o + p / s − p / s′`; from the opening view, the pointer at (440, 340) over (400, 300), zoomed to 2, the origin is (180, 130), (400, 300) still under the pointer (`zoomedView`).

### The history of states

How the history is built and measured (ADR-0095, accepted on 2026-10-23, the choice left to the agent by the Product Owner; SP-029, `src/model/history/`):

- **States.** The history holds the drawing's past states, its present and its states to come, as Redux's `{ past, present, future }`; a state is the drawing, each shape as the page keeps it, and its selection, the indices of the shapes selected.
- **A step.** Each finished action records the drawing and the selection: the drawing first shares with the present each shape whose JSON text is a present shape's, taking the present's object, the one at the same index when its text is the same, so that two shapes of equal texts read again unchanged are the present's two (`sharedDrawing`, AUD-042); every shape the same object as the present's, in the same order, is the same drawing, which takes no step, the present's selection replaced, past and future kept, so that selecting alone, or a value typed back as it was, never makes one; any other drawing, a shape changed, added, deleted or moved in the order, pushes the present onto the past, becomes the present, and drops the states to come.
- **The selection** (SP-029's answer 2, 2026-10-23): a state keeps the selection as it was when its step was taken, the selection followed while it changes alone; undo gives back the selection before the step, redo the one after it, as Penpot restores them, so that an undo shows what it changed: A′ and B, B selected, then B deleted, undo brings B back selected, redo leaves A′, none selected.
- **Undo and redo.** Undo makes the last past state the present, the present the first to come; redo the other way; with nothing past, or nothing to come, the history is unchanged.
- **Its size**, in bytes, a count of the data and not the engine's memory: for each shape a past or future state holds and the present does not, once, its JSON text's length times 2, a string being 16-bit code units; plus 8 for each reference to a shape and each index of a selection the past and future states hold, the slot of a tagged value without pointer compression, an upper bound, V8 holding it in 4 bytes with compression (`REF-V8-POINTER-COMPRESSION`). A rectangle 100 × 50 kept by the page is 614 characters: A nudged once, A selected, the history holds A, 1 228 bytes, two references, 16, and one index, 8, 1 252 bytes; nothing past nor to come is 0.
- **Its size kept** (EN-147, ADR-0097, agent's choice): the history holds, beside its states, its size and, for each shape, how many past and future states hold it, each state counting a shape once, so that a step, an undo, a redo and the bound update the size from the states and the shapes they move, never counting the history again. A state added to the past or to the states to come holds each of its shapes once more, one held by none before and not in the present adding its text's length times 2, and adds 8 for each reference and index; a state removed, the reverse, one held by it alone and not in the present taking its text away; the present changed, each shape it leaves that a state holds adds its text, each it takes that a state holds takes it away, a selection replaced alone changing nothing. A step adds the present to the past, removes each state to come, then changes the present; an undo removes the last past state, adds the present to the states to come, then changes the present to it; a redo the other way. A nudged from A and B, A selected: A and B added to the past, both in the present, 3 × 8 = 24 bytes, then A left by the present, held by 1, 1 228: 1 252, the size counted again. The counts are kept so that each history keeps its own and a step copies only the counts it changes, whatever the history holds (`sizedHistoryWithStep`, `sizedUndoneHistory`, `sizedRedoneHistory`).
- **Its bound** (SP-029's answers 6 and 7, 2026-10-23): a memory size and a number of steps kept whatever their size, both set by the designer, 100 MB (100 000 000 bytes) and 5 steps by default, the size read by the agent from a view of up to about 1 000 symbols kept pleasant to use, the 5 steps GIMP's default. Beyond the size, the oldest past states are dropped first, the fewest that bring it within the size, never leaving fewer past states than the steps kept, as GIMP; a step larger than the whole size is kept while within the steps kept. The states to come count in the size but the bound never drops them: the next step does. A move of A alone adds 1 244 bytes, A as it was, its reference and its index, so that 12 440 bytes keep 10 moves of it, the eleventh dropping the first, and 10 MB 8 038; in a drawing of 100 rectangles, a move of A adds 2 036 bytes, its state's 100 references 800. With the size kept, the oldest past states are dropped one by one while the size is over the size set, each read once, so that a history within the size is bounded by comparing two numbers and one over it by a step's state reads that state alone (`sizedBoundedHistory`, EN-147, agent's choice); counted again, the fewest states to drop are found by doubling the count tried from 1, then halving the range left, a history within the size measured once and one over it by a step's state twice, the same history (`boundedHistory`, agent's choice).
- **The moves held** (SP-029's answer 6): about how many moves of one shape a memory size holds, the size over the 1 244 bytes of a move of A alone, rounded down to tens: 100 MB, 100 000 000 / 1 244 = 80 385.9, about 80 380; 20 MB, 16 070; 0, 0 (`movesWithinSize`, agent's choice).
- **The list of steps** (US-077, SP-029's answer 8, 2026-10-23; the names, the window and the move the agent's choices, F41 having no validation of its own): the states the history holds, the oldest first, as GIMP lists the actions most recently performed and goes back to one by a click (`REF-GIMP-UNDO-DIALOG`). Each state is named by the step that led to it, read from the drawings before and after it, what records the step, so that no place changing the drawing names its action (ADR-0095): shapes added, every shape before kept, the same objects in the same order, `Add`; shapes removed so, `Delete`; as many shapes, one of them an object the drawing before held at another index, shapes moved in the order, A and B swapped or B sent to the top, `Change`, the shapes compared index by index being then two shapes (`isReordered`, AUD-043); as many shapes otherwise, the fields of each shape replaced by another object at its index, compared by their JSON texts, the kind the panel shows among them (`stepName`): `kind` among them, `Convert`; only `x` and `y`, `Move`; one field beside `x` and `y`, the panel's label of that field, its unit or hint in brackets left out, so that a width typed while the anchor moves the shape stays `Width` (`fieldsStepName`, `fieldLabel`; agent's choice, 2026-10-23): `isItalic` `Italic`, `isUnderlined` `Underline`, `isStruckThrough` `Strike-through`, `strikeThroughColor` `Strike-through color`, a polygon's `radii` `Radius` and `stretched` `Stretched to its box`, a text box's `boxWidth` `Width`, `textCase` `Case`, `maximumLines` `Max lines` (`FIELD_LABELS`), and a field whose label is its name in words, split before each capital, the first letter a capital, `width` `Width`, `letterSpacing` `Letter spacing` (`fieldWords`); no field, several others, or any other step, `Change`. The oldest state the history holds, no step before it kept, is `Start`. A nudged, B deleted and 150 typed in A's W: `Move`, `Delete`, `Width`, the last the present.
- **The window of the list** (US-077, agent's choice): at most 15 rows, the present always, then of the 14 beside it at most half, 7, states to come after it and the rest past before it, the side holding fewer states giving its rows to the other, and the states left out counted on each side, so that the list costs its 15 rows whatever the history holds (`shownHistoryWindow`, `historySteps`): Start and 20 moves, the last present, show `6 earlier steps`, then 14 moves and the present; 6 undone, 8 past moves, the present and the 6 to come, after `6 earlier steps`; back at the 7th state of the 21, Start and 5 moves, the present, then 8 to come, the 6 past leaving their rows to them, before `6 later steps`.
- **Going to a step** (US-077, agent's choice): a click on a row makes its state the present by as many undos as states after it are past, or as many redos as states to come up to it, so that each undo and redo keeps the size and the selection (§7, Its size kept; The selection) and the steps after it are kept to redo; a step taken then drops them, as an undo followed by a change does (ADR-0095); the present, or a state the history no longer holds, leaves the history as it is (`sizedHistoryAt`). A nudged, B deleted, 150 typed in W, then Move clicked: A nudged at its width before, B back, Delete and Width to redo; A nudged again, Delete and Width dropped.

### Undo and redo in the playground

How the page records, undoes and redoes the drawing's actions and the panel's changes (US-074, US-075, SP-029's answers 2, 3, 4, 8 and 9, 2026-10-23; the agent's choices marked so, for the review's summary, F40 having no validation of its own):

- **Where a step is taken.** At each click on the drawing, each release of the pointer on it, a drag the browser cancels included, and each key released outside the panel's fields, the page records what it shows once it is redrawn from the action (ADR-0095, decision 3): a shape placed by a click, drawn by a drag, shapes moved by a drag, deleted, nudged, each make one step, and a click, a selection rectangle, Ctrl + A or Escape, the drawing unchanged, only the selection kept with the present (Q7). A drag cancelled gives the drawing back, so takes none (agent's choice).
- **A key held** is one step from its press to its release (SP-029's answer 3): Right held, 30 keys sent, A from (10, 20) to (40, 20), then Right pressed once more, (41, 20); Ctrl + Z gives (40, 20), then (10, 20).
- **The panel's changes** (US-075, SP-029's answer 4): a step is asked at each value applied in the panel, by Enter or by leaving its field (`change`, `REF-MDN-CHANGE-EVENT`), at each choice — the anchor, Stretched, a handle's mode, a text's weight, its fit —, at each click on one of its buttons — a turn, Convert to free shape, Add a point, Delete points, the handles, Bold, Italic, the box's Reset —, and in the color picker at each key released on a slider or the plane and each release of the pointer on them, a key held one step from its press to its release, as on the drawing; for one shape, several (F38), the points, the handles and a text alike. - **A text placed and typed** (SP-029's answer 5): from its placing, by a click or a drag, until its Content field is left with a content typed, no step is taken, so that the text placed and what is typed in it, its size and its options set meanwhile, are one step, taken as the field is left; Ctrl + Z then removes it, nothing being there before: the Text tool clicked at (250, 140), `Valve` typed and the field left, one Ctrl + Z removes the text, Ctrl + Shift + Z brings `Valve` back; a content typed later in a text already there is a step of its own, as any value applied.
