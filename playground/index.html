Words

The radius says how much a corner is rounded: it is the radius of the circle drawn in the corner. On a rectangle the curve starts that many pixels before the corner; on a polygon it starts sooner at sharp corners, later at wide ones. The requested radius is the number you type; it is kept. The effective radius is the one drawn: smaller when the shape is too small for the request. The rectangle is a free shape: its corners are kept in a box of their own and stretched to the width and height you type, so a width of 0 then back gives the same shape. Each corner has its own radius; when they differ, the Radius field is empty and reads Mixed, and a number typed there rounds every corner. A polygon has 3 to 12 equal sides and a flat base; it fills its width × height box without being stretched. The grey rectangle is that box. The Points mode, chosen with the large button under the guided test, shows each corner of the shape as a round handle: the red one is point 1, where the outline starts, and the green one point 2, which shows the way it goes. Click a handle, or a row of the list of points, to select that point; Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) + click adds or removes one, Shift + click in the list selects a range. The Radius field then rounds the selected points only. The anchor says where the polygon sits in the room the box leaves: one of nine places, from top left to bottom right; the center by default. Sizes are screen pixels: a value above 100 000 becomes 100 000.
